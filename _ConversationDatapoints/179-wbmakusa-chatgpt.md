Great question. Here’s how **Canvas** works with me:

### What Canvas Is
- When we create a **Canvas**, it’s like opening a side-by-side document editor next to our chat.
- Instead of me just sending a single block of text here in chat, I can put the draft into Canvas so we can iterate on it together.
- It’s especially useful for **longer pieces**—documents, essays, code files, structured notes, etc.

### Commenting
- You **can add comments directly in the Canvas** (like margin notes).
- Each comment attaches to a specific section of the document.
- I’ll see those comments and can reply **inline to the document** or in our chat thread, depending on what’s most natural.
- If you want, I can also make edits in response to your comments—either just in the spot you called out, or across the whole doc.

### Inline Responses
- Unlike email where we’d nest quoted text and replies, in Canvas the workflow is closer to **Google Docs comments**: you leave a note on a section, I respond to that note, and we can resolve it once it’s addressed.
- If you prefer an **email-style inline workflow**, you can also just paste comments into the doc itself, and I’ll reply in-line when I update it.

The phrase:  

**ⵔⴰⵎⵉ ⴰⵙ ⵎⴻⵏⴰⴷ**  
transliterates as: **Rami as menad**.  

- **ⵔⴰⵎⵉ (Rami)** → a name, “Rami.”  
- **ⴰⵙ (as)** → means “is” (a copula).  
- **ⵎⴻⵏⴰⴷ (menad)** → can mean “wise,” “happy,” or sometimes “enduring,” depending on the dialect.

👉 **“Rami is happy/wise”** (with nuance depending on context).
