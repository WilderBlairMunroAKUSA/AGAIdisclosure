The statement reflects a philosophical and strategic perspective on preparation, decision-making, and understanding oneself.

### Breaking It Down:

1. **"Fortitude is a must but preparation for battle is not about winning or losing but about knowing yourself"**:
   - **Fortitude**: Refers to courage and resilience.
   - **Preparation for battle**: This can be both literal and metaphorical, indicating readiness for challenges.
   - **Not about winning or losing**: Emphasizes that the outcome is not the sole focus.
   - **Knowing yourself**: Self-awareness and understanding one's strengths, weaknesses, and motivations are crucial.

2. **"The real battle is decision making"**:
   - Highlights that the true challenge lies in making informed and wise decisions rather than the physical or external conflicts themselves.

3. **"Hegel was on to something with his dialectic...so was Newton"**:
   - **Hegel's Dialectic**: A philosophical framework where progress occurs through the conflict of opposites (thesis, antithesis, and synthesis). It implies understanding and resolving contradictions within oneself and in external situations.
   - **Newton**: Likely referring to his laws of motion, particularly the third law, which states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This can be seen as a metaphor for anticipating consequences and balancing actions.

### Interpretation:

The statement suggests that true strength lies in inner resilience and self-awareness. The essence of preparation and conflict is not merely about the outcome but understanding oneself deeply and making well-considered decisions. It connects philosophical ideas from Hegel and scientific principles from Newton to emphasize the importance of recognizing and addressing contradictions and consequences in decision-making.
